Inside a reading desk
that runs itself

Most of a clerk’s October is spent before the billing system is ever opened: deciphering a photo, comparing it with last year, asking for the digit that is missing. This is what happens when software takes those steps and leaves the clerk the decisions.

Key takeaways

  • The time goes into the steps before billing, not into billing itself.
  • A tool that reads a photo and then asks a clerk to type the number has saved nobody any time.
  • Start with one document type. At Stadtwerke Lahn, 94% of readings now book with zero edits across 64,000 meters.
  • If your meters already report by themselves, you do not need this.

Where the eleven minutes go

None of this shows up in a system report. It lives in what experienced clerks know: one landlord photographs three meters at once, one bakery always reads a day late, one address disputes every bill.

Counting the reply and the correction, a reading takes eleven minutes of a clerk’s time. In October there are 40,000 of them.

When you should not buy this

Smart meters make most of this unnecessary. So does low volume: with fewer than a few hundred readings a month there is too little repetition for the system to learn from, and the time saved would not fill one working week a year. Hire a temp instead. We would say the same on a call.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which process should go first?

The one with the most daily volume and the fewest special cases. It shows within two weeks whether reading, checking and booking work on your data.

How is this different from OCR?

OCR turns an image into characters and fails when the form changes. This checks what it read against your records and then acts on it.
